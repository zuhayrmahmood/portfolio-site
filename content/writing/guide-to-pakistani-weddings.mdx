Pakistani weddings are a unique celebration of love, family and culture. Having attended quite a few in my life,
I sort of consider myself an *unofficial* expert.

First things first, Pakistani weddings *(I'm just going to say weddings from now on for simplicity)* are almost never a party hosted by the bride and groom, but rather a 
party hosted by their parents. The entire wedding process is a **multi-day journey**,
with each event being unique. I'll break up the events into two categories: pre-wedding and wedding events. Ordered chronologically.

# Pre-Wedding Events

## Baat Pakki / Mangni
For the non-urdu speaking folk reading this, Baat Pakki translates to "the deal is confirmed". This usually
isn't a really formal event but it's an intimate celebration with family and close friends. 
I decided to group it with the Mangni, which means "engagement" since oftentimes, these two are intertwined. Needless to say though,
some families might prefer to have separate events for the two, but given how often they're combined, I thought it'd be better 
to group them here. 

Typically, the Mangni is a slightly more formal event than the Baat Pakki, but they're both pretty casual overall.
If you're going to a Mangni event, you can expect to see the bride and groom exchange rings and in traditional Pakistani attire. 

## Dua-e-khair

This is a less popular event that is typically hosted for the well-being of the future couple. Community members will gather and pray for the couple's future
and for their marriage to be blessed. Since there's a religious aspect to the event, there typically isn't any music or any dancing *(especially at the time of the [dua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua) itself)*.

## Dholki

Dholki's are a special event since there typically isn't just a single one, there are multiple
dholki events. During the dholki(s), these events are typically hosted individually by the bride and groom's sides.
The dholki events are comprised of traditional Pakistani folk music, with the [dholki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholak) being the main instrument.
Women will often gather around in a circle and sing many traditional songs *(my favourite is ["Kala Shah Kala"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xug-ZehSkSc), a song which fights against colorism in south asian culture)*.

Dholkis are semi-formal events where men and women usually wear [shalwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_kameez) or [lehengas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehenga). The vibe is prety casual and 
it's supposed to be a fun, chaotic event where everyone is (usually) participating.
