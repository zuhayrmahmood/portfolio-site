Pakistani weddings are a unique celebration of love, family and culture. Having attended quite a few in my life,
I sort of consider myself an *unofficial* expert.

First things first, Pakistani weddings *(I'm just going to say weddings from now on for simplicity)* are almost never a party hosted by the bride and groom, but rather a 
party hosted by their parents. The entire wedding process is a **multi-day journey**,
with each event being unique. I'll break up the events into two categories: pre-wedding and wedding events. Ordered chronologically.

# Pre-Wedding Events

## Baat Pakki / Mangni
For the non-urdu speaking folk reading this, Baat Pakki translates to "the deal is confirmed". This usually
isn't a really formal event but it's an intimate celebration with family and close friends. 
I decided to group it with the Mangni, which means "engagement" since oftentimes, these two are intertwined. Needless to say though,
some families might prefer to have separate events for the two, but given how often they're combined, I thought it'd be better 
to group them here. 

Typically, the Mangni is a slightly more formal event than the Baat Pakki, but they're both pretty casual overall.
If you're going to a Mangni event, you can expect to see the bride and groom exchange rings and in traditional Pakistani attire. 

## Dua-e-khair

This is a less popular event that is typically hosted for the well-being of the future couple. Community members will gather and pray for the couple's future
and for their marriage to be blessed. Since there's a religious aspect to the event, there typically isn't any music or any dancing *(especially at the time of the [dua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua) itself)*.

## Dholki

Dholki's are a special event since there typically isn't just a single one, there are multiple
dholki events for a single wedding. During the dholki(s), these events are typically hosted separately, meaning the bride and groom will have their own dholkis.
Usually, these events are hosted by the friends and family of the to-be-weds. 
Dholkis are comprised of traditional Pakistani folk music, sung by women with a small [dholak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholak) being the main instrument. Yes, the name of the event and the instrument are the same *(very confusing sometimes, I know)*.
Women will often gather around in a circle and sing many traditional songs *(yes, I have a favourite, you have to ask me to find out lol)*.

Dholkis are semi-formal events where men and women usually wear [shalwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_kameez) or [lehengas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehenga). The vibe is prety casual and 
it's supposed to be a fun, chaotic event where everyone is *usually* participating. 
## Mayoun

The Mayoun ceremony is pretty interesting since it originally stems from predominantly Punjabi roots, but has changed over time and is slightly different for different groups of people. For example, some communities
will rub *haldi* (tumeric) on the bride/groom, some will just put some *mehndi* (henna) on a leaf in the bride/groom's hands. The way I've mostly seen it done is the following:
- Bride/groom do it separately, with their own circles
- Usually the bride will wear yellow on this day
- Bride/groom will have a leaf in their hands where guests will put mehndi on it
- Sweets will be fed to the to-be-wed, since sweets are a symbol of happiness (they will beg you to give them a small amount since everyone is giving them sweets, the choice is yours)

In the past, the Mayoun has typically been a glorified before and after of the bride/groom. In other communities, the to-be-wed, will sometimes have tumeric rubbed on their body and face, they'll look a bit disheveled and sometimes the bride won't wear makeup.
This will contrast with the wedding events when they walk in and they are "glowing" and look amazing compared to the Mayoun.

# Wedding Events
We finally make it to the weddnig events! These are the core events that makeup a shaadi. One thing to keep in mind, is that sometimes weddings will have a mishmash of a few days in one. So the traditions that happen on a certain day might all be in a single day. 
This is the most fun part of any wedding *(and the most stressful if you're hosting)*. 
## Mehndi
In my humble opinion, the Mehndi is the most fun event of a wedding. Depending on religious inclinations, the Mehndi can be pretty different across hosts. Some people won't have much music, won't have dancing, and it'll mostly be a gathering where the bride and groom are celebrated.
Other times, the Mehndi can be a full-blown party with a DJ, photobooth, and lots of dancing. The Mehndi is typically hosted by the bride's family *(and finally, the groom will be there)*. 

Mehndis are typically really colourful, with green, yellow, orange and sometimes pink dominating.
